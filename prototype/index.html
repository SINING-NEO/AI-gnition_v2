A retention agent that only says what the orders can prove.

Customer histories go in. A small neural net estimates when the next purchase is due. An agent decides whether to write, and a critic throws the message away if it invents a product.

Where this is

Feasibility is done on synthetic data. The prototype below is the first time the pieces run as one loop. Nothing has been emailed to a real person.

Done

  • A filter that tells a consumable catalogue from a fashion-like one.
  • A sequence network that beats a median-gap rule when recent products change the timing.
  • A critic that rejects products the customer never bought.

This prototype

  • Five synthetic people walk the full loop.
  • Send, holdout, missing consent, and stay-quiet each happen once or twice.
  • Replies are scripted: buy, or later.

Not built yet

  • A real order export.
  • A live language model in the writer seat.
  • The uplift model. It needs the holdout log this loop is designed to write.

The system, in one pass

Deep learning here is the score: given this person’s past orders, how many days until they buy again? The agent is the procedure around that score. It calls tools, picks one job, and stops when a reply or a rule says to stop.

The language model, when we add one, only writes the sentence and reads the reply. It does not choose who is contacted. That choice has to stay random for a slice of people, or we can never learn whether the message caused the purchase.

Ordinary code still owns two gates: consent, and the holdout coin. A fluent model is the wrong place for either decision.

1 · TimelineOrders, dates, consent. No new questions asked of the customer.
2 · Sequence modelA two-layer network reads the last two products and the gaps, and predicts days.
3 · PlannerOne job only: replenish, lapse, or stay quiet.
4 · PolicyConsent check, then a 20% holdout. The probability is stored.
5 · WriterOne sentence from the brief. The prototype uses a template, not a live model.
6 · CriticEvery product and date in the sentence must be in the timeline.
7 · ReplyBuy, later, or stop. Scripted in this prototype.
8 · MemoryThe row waits for a purchase within 14 days, which is what an uplift model will train on.

What we measured before this loop

Days of error predicting the next gap

Lower is better. 400 held-out synthetic customers in each world. The network is a stand-in for a larger sequence model: if this small net cannot beat the median, a transformer does not get the job.

Who even qualifies for replenishment

A regular gap is a weak test. Requiring a repeated product and a 14–45 day cycle separates the two catalogues.

Sources: feasibility/sequence_model.py and feasibility/replenishment.mjs, seed 19. The critic in that probe rejected 214 of 214 invented products and kept all 186 real ones.

The words on this page

Sequence model

A network trained on past orders to predict the next gap. This prototype uses two hidden layers of 16 units. It sees the last two products, whether they match, the last gap, and the person’s median gap.

Agent

The loop that calls the timeline, the model, the writer, and the critic, then updates memory from the reply. The model scores. The agent acts.

Critic

A checker, not a second writer. If the sentence names a product or a date the timeline tool did not return, or adds a discount, the draft is rejected.

Holdout

A random slice of eligible people who get no message. Their later purchases are the comparison that tells us whether writing caused the repeat buy.

Uplift model

A later network with two heads: chance they buy if we write, and chance they buy if we do not. It cannot be trained until this loop has logged both kinds of decision.

Propensity

The probability the policy used when it assigned send or holdout. Storing it is what makes the later uplift training valid.
